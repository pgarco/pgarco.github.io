\
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as annotation and labeling tools in political science research, particularly for content analysis tasks involving sensitive political material. This paper examines how over-safety mechanisms embedded in LLMs can systematically distort the labels produced by these models when applied to politically sensitive content. To demonstrate this argument empirically, I propose Congressional floor speeches as the hardest-possible-case corpus for safety refusals to be justified, given its public-domain status, official legislative context, and unambiguous party-affiliation ground truth. The paper calls for political scientists to treat LLM-generated labels for sensitive political content\
as theoretically bias data unless explicit measures are taken to audit and correct for safety-induced suppression\
